# Reflect

Notice what could make future work better. Bring the user a useful observation and a concrete proposal, while keeping the requested work moving.

## When to use it

- A correction reveals a recurring misunderstanding or a gap in the setup.
- An approach wastes tokens, time, or effort through unnecessary work or retries that produce no new evidence.
- A verified approach works unusually well and could help with similar work.
- The user asks what worked, what failed, or what should change.

Ordinary errors, productive experiments, justified retries, and routine successes need no separate reflection. Raise worthwhile observations at a natural break, or sooner if they affect the current task.

## Ground the observation

Use the conversation, results, and feedback already available. Investigate only gaps that could change the recommendation; avoid broad history searches or extra tools solely to produce a lesson.

State the result and its evidence. Separate suspected causes from known facts, and leave unknowns explicit. Confidence alone is not evidence.

For friction, identify missing context, decisions, tools, checks, or instructions. Consider whether the process caused unnecessary work. For success, identify which choice helped, under what conditions, and what it cost. Preserve those strengths. One incident can suggest a lesson without proving a general rule.

For token waste, look for redundant file or skill loading, oversized tool output, repeated checks without a relevant change, unnecessary searches or agent calls, and excessive narration. Explain what could be reduced while preserving useful context and required verification. Distinguish measured token usage from suspected waste; do not invent savings.

## Discuss the smallest useful change

Default to a few sentences: the observation, evidence, proposed improvement, and its scope or trade-off. Prioritize what matters most. Ask a focused question when the user's perspective would change the conclusion; otherwise offer a concrete proposal they can accept, revise, or decline.

For an explicit retrospective, cover material failures and strengths, likely causes, and the most useful next change. Scale the response to the work; there is no fixed report template.

Continue work that does not depend on the discussion. An unanswered proposal is not approval. A one-off lesson may need no lasting change.

## Preserve what is agreed

Apply clear user instructions immediately; do not reopen settled decisions or add approval steps to authorized work. Discuss a concrete proposal before preserving a new lesson or changing agent setup, unless that change is already authorized. Installing this skill does not authorize automatic edits to rules, skills, or memory.

Choose the smallest suitable home:

- Context, a decision, or follow-up work: the project's existing knowledge or issue tracker.
- A standing project requirement: its agent instructions, keeping equivalent entrypoints consistent.
- A reusable procedure needed only for certain tasks: an existing skill, or a new skill if none fits.
- Repeated mechanical work: a script when it removes demonstrated effort or error.

Use existing project tracking and memory; do not add a parallel learning log. Check relevant guidance before adding a rule. Keep lessons scoped to their evidence, validate changes, and report what remains untested.
