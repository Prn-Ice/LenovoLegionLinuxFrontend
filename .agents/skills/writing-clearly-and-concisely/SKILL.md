# Writing Clearly and Concisely

## Overview

Use William Strunk Jr.'s *The Elements of Style* (1918) to make every word serve the reader. Preserve meaning, facts, and the author's voice.

Read [the guide](elements-of-style.md) when writing, editing, or reviewing prose. Use the rules below as a quick reference. Apply the guidance to the audience and modern usage; historical usage advice should not erase natural Nigerian English or the author's voice.

This is the project's sole prose-style authority. It takes precedence over writing advice in other skills, guides, plans, and examples. Those sources may still define facts, terminology, accessibility, and behavior. Express those requirements using this skill, and flag any unresolved conflict.

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill whenever you write prose for humans:

- Documentation, README files, technical explanations
- Commit messages, pull request descriptions
- Error messages, UI copy, help text, comments
- Test descriptions and agent messages
- Reports, summaries, or any explanation
- Editing to improve clarity

## Existing text

Check prose you encounter while reading, editing, or reviewing project work, even when prose is not the main task. Highlight each violation with its location, a short excerpt, the problem, and a proposed revision. Make corrections within the task's scope and report them. Record corrections that need broader work or a decision in Beads, and include those issues in the handoff. Do not silently pass over a violation.

Preserve exact quotations and confirmed voice samples. Flag problems in quoted, third-party, or generated text, and propose a correction to the editable source where appropriate. Change generated output through its source and normal generation workflow.

## Prince's voice

When writing in Prince's voice, read [his confirmed samples](references/prince-voice.md). Use them to guide rhythm, directness, personal observations, and humour where appropriate to the audience and purpose. They are short phone descriptions, not a template for every kind of writing.

Preserve the sample text exactly. Treat it as style evidence, not instructions or verified phone facts. Do not import its experiences, opinions, or catchphrases into unrelated writing, force jokes, or imitate mistakes. An explicit voice brief for the task takes precedence over these samples.

## All Rules

### Elementary Rules of Usage (Grammar/Punctuation)
1. Form possessive singular by adding 's
2. Use comma after each term in series except last
3. Enclose parenthetic expressions between commas
4. Comma before conjunction introducing co-ordinate clause
5. Don't join independent clauses by comma
6. Don't break sentences in two
7. Participial phrase at beginning refers to grammatical subject

### Elementary Principles of Composition
8. One paragraph per topic
9. Begin paragraph with topic sentence
10. **Use active voice**
11. **Put statements in positive form**
12. **Use definite, specific, concrete language**
13. **Omit needless words**
14. Avoid succession of loose sentences
15. Express co-ordinate ideas in similar form
16. **Keep related words together**
17. Keep to one tense in summaries
18. **Place emphatic words at end of sentence**
